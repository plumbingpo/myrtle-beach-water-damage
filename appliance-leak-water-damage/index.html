Appliance Leak Water Damage in Myrtle Beach: What to Know

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Myrtle Beach, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Rooms Next Door

A washer hose can release water for hours. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

How We Fix It

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. Where water is trapped under cabinets or floating floors, we remove toe kicks or lift boards to reach it.

Surfaces touched by washer or dishwasher discharge get antimicrobial treatment. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
