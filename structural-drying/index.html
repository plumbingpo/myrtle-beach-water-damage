No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Myrtle Beach Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Myrtle Beach

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
