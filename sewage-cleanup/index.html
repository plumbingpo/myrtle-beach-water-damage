Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
