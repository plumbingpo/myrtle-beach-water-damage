Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Myrtle Beach

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. Sagging changes the picture. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Signs to Look For

  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Joint tape coming loose, with screws and panel edges telegraphing through
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Electricity Overhead

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Damage on the Floor Above

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.
