Restoration Challenges Charleston Homeowners Face

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Charleston households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Charleston. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Myrtle Beach Restoration crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.

Insurance and Water Damage: A Guide for Charleston Owners

Adjusters work from evidence, so the strongest thing a Charleston homeowner can bring to a water damage claim is a good record. Start building it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away.

Get pictures of whatever failed, whether a hose, a valve or a pipe joint, and hang on to the part itself. Shoot every room from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of ruined belongings and the model numbers on electrical goods. Hold damaged flooring and possessions until your adjuster has inspected them or said that pictures are enough. Many policies treat an abrupt failure differently from long-term seepage or flood water coming in from outdoors. What to Do Before Our Charleston Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

In every case, there are simple ways to save the crew time when it reaches Charleston. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Write down when you found the water and what you think caused it. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Do not assume clean water will remain that way. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a Charleston leak seems small.

How Charleston Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in Charleston homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. If you find something in your Charleston home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.
